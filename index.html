Expanso and Jev: examples

Eleven pipelines where Expanso Edge handles what rules can handle and asks Jev, TypeSafe's structured classifier, only about the rest. Each one has a page that steps through a real run, one stage at a time, with the actual message going in and coming out.

Every pipeline here runs on its shipped input with uv run -s tools/fixture-runner.py run; the latest dated report is 2026-10-05-fixture-run.md.

  1. 01. Log triage

    Most log lines are routine and a rule can say so instantly. A few are not, and no rule you have written yet knows which. Expanso shapes, fingerprints and counts every record, sends known routine lines straight to the archive, and asks Jev only about what is left.

    Step through itREADME

  2. 02. Support ticket router

    Every ticket gets a department, an intent, a frustration score and an urgency judgment before a person reads it. Confident urgency pages on-call, strong frustration jumps the line, a weak department match waits for a human, and everything else goes to its department queue.

    Step through itREADME

  3. 03. Sensitivity masking

    Each record is classified for sensitivity and checked for personal data and credentials before it leaves the source. The matching policy is applied on the spot: pass, mask, tokenize or quarantine.

    Step through itREADME

  4. 04. Sensor triage

    Every reading is judged against its asset's normal range, in context rather than as a raw number. A real anomaly dispatches maintenance with the reading attached; routine telemetry rolls up into aggregates.

    Step through itREADME

  5. 05. Agent guardrail

    A safety check on every tool call an AI agent makes. Calls that match what the user asked for pass; risky or off-intent calls are blocked or held for approval. Uncertainty fails closed.

    Step through itREADME

  6. 06. Smart sampling

    Every event is scored for how interesting it is. Interesting events are all kept; routine noise is sampled at one in a hundred. How much volume this saves depends on how noisy your source is.

    Step through itREADME

  7. 07. SOC pre-filter

    Authentication and access events get a threat class and an escalation judgment at the edge. Only real escalations reach the SIEM; everything else lands in cheaper storage with its scores attached.

    Step through itREADME

  8. 08. Data quality firewall

    Rows headed for the warehouse are judged for quality and schema conformance at the edge. Bad rows are quarantined with the judgment attached; good rows flow through.

    Step through itREADME

  9. 09. Moderation pre-filter

    User content gets a category judgment before anyone sees it. Safe content is allowed automatically; everything else queues for a person, most uncertain first.

    Step through itREADME

  10. 10. Feedback miner

    Every review and survey comment gets a topic, a sentiment score and a churn-risk judgment as it arrives. Churn risks alert the account team, confident bug reports go to engineering, and everything feeds analytics.
